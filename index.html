Projects

Backward Reconstruction of Initial Vorticity Fields Using Frequency-Domain Neural Operators (University of Pennsylvania)

Duration: November 2025 – December 2025

Role: Project Leader, Department of Mathematics

Description: Investigated the backward reconstruction of initial vorticity fields for two-dimensional advection–diffusion systems, an intrinsically ill-posed inverse problem due to diffusion-induced information loss and advective mixing. Constructed a large-scale synthetic dataset consisting of 2000 high-resolution numerical trajectories generated from a discretized PDE solver. Designed and implemented a variable-step inverse operator learning framework to stabilize backward-time inference and mitigate long-horizon error accumulation. Conducted a systematic comparison between Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) and frequency-domain Vision Transformer architectures, analyzing reconstruction accuracy, spectral stability, and robustness to noise. Demonstrated that spectral inductive bias is critical for inverse PDE learning, with FNO producing stable, physically meaningful reconstructions while Transformer-based models suffer severe high-frequency amplification.

Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg Transformer for Robust Small-Target Feature Representation (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)

Duration: July 2024 – November 2024

Role: Research Intern, School of Computer Science and Technology

Description: Designed and implemented a Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg Transformer model that integrates the long-range dependency modeling capabilities of Transformer architectures with the local feature extraction strengths of convolutional neural networks, improving representation quality for small targets in complex environments. Introduced a random connection attention module to replace standard self-attention, enabling sparse attention learning that significantly reduced computational and memory overhead while maintaining or improving model accuracy. Further enhanced model robustness by stacking specially designed Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg Transformer modules, leveraging numerical integration principles to suppress noise, stabilize feature propagation, and improve performance under challenging conditions.
